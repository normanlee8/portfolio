Overview

Users
Recruiters first, then me, then anyone who lands here. A recruiter might give a portfolio a minute or less.
Challenge
Get people to where they need to get to as quickly as possible, while still feeling like me: simple, clean, and a few small details I find cool.
Constraints
Live by the end of October. No frameworks. Every device: phones, tablets, and computers.

Motion Without the Wait

Keeping the fun parts, and cutting what slowed the site down.

The project covers move: the portfolio cover scrolls a wall of the site's sections, and the Apple Sports cover lifts parts of a phone screen. I kept them, but they only come alive when someone points at or tabs to a project; at rest, each is a still picture of itself.

Switching pages used to slide the Apple Sports cover across the screen into the next page. It made every page change feel slower, so I removed it: pages now simply fade in, quickly. The home page keeps its one-by-one entrance as the first impression, made snappier, and the theme switch got a short, subtle fade, since instant felt abrupt and a slow fade felt unresponsive.

Early Concepts

From a first sketch, to an experiment, and back to clean.

I started minimal, then tried lined paper, which works on light and dark backgrounds for the theme toggle I wanted, on my family's table with sticky-note tabs as navigation. I dropped it: too much going on, text was hard to fit, and paper is too tall for a wide screen. A blue gradient was clean but not neutral, so it went too.

Easy on the Eyes

Neutral colors, in light and dark.

Not everyone likes any single color, so the site uses an off-white and a gray. People differ on light and dark mode, so it opens in the device's theme, with a toggle in the header. Neither theme is pure white or pure black, which are harsh on the eyes.

Choosing the Words

A title I can back up, and a clear headline.

I changed my title from Product Designer to User Experience Designer, since I had nothing yet to back up "product" and making lives easier is what I care about most. "Make life easier." beat "Designing paths of least resistance": the same idea, but punchier and less confusing.

From Figma to Code

Designed at three sizes, then built component by component.

I designed every page at 393, 768, and 1440px with auto layout, which maps almost one to one onto CSS flexbox. I built the site myself in plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. In code, new breakpoints appeared at 1024 and 1280px, where the tablet layout looked odd.

In hindsightDesigning for more widths up front would have saved reworking layouts in code.

  • On this page

    Jump to any section of a case study.

  • Return to top

    One click back up a long page.

    Light or dark, starting from the device's setting.
